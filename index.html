01 / Find your people

Start with a real problem.

The best content starts with an audience you understand. Pick one person, one frustrating moment, and one useful thing your app can do.

Explore the branches

One person

Describe the moment

Define the person, their situation, and their immediate frustration. “A student with an exam tomorrow” is more actionable than “young people.”

02 / Content that clicks

Three formats. Plenty to test.

Start with a familiar structure. Change the problem, the opening line, or the demonstration to learn what makes your audience lean in.

Explore the branches

Content that clicks

One useful idea, three ways in

01

Hook & demo

Personal hook → visible payoff

Begin face-to-camera with a relatable problem, then demonstrate the app solving it. Show the useful action and result rather than listing features.

V2 · 05:56
Example: the exam tomorrow

Original angle for a study app: “My exam is tomorrow and my notes look like this.” Show the notes, make a practice quiz, then answer one question.

02

Group-chat text

Short clip + conversational text

The notes suggest roughly six-second clips with longer text overlays. Write something people could actually send a friend, and check that it is readable.

V2 · 05:56
Example: the language freeze

Original angle: “Does anyone else understand the whole conversation in their head and then forget every word when it’s their turn?” Pair it with a simple practice clip.

03

Tell the story

Situation → friction → discovery

Work the product into a relatable story. Use an honest experience and a concrete result, with the app playing a useful role.

V2 · 05:56
Example: a week that got away

For a planning app, describe an actual week that became overwhelming, then show the small planning habit that helped. This is an original example; use your real experience.

04

Creative habits

Human voice, tools backstage

The PlayKit notes favor human connection over fake-looking AI creative. Use AI for operational support such as vetting or training.

03 / Build a creator team

Teammates, not a content factory.

Find people who understand the audience, can use feedback, and care about the product. Give them a system that helps them do good work.

Explore the branches

A creator team

A system that supports real people

01

Start in-house

Learn before you delegate

Understand the app’s message yourself. The notes recommend bringing in an agency once you have a format that works.

02

Paid trial

A week to test the fit

Use a week-long paid trial before a long-term commitment. Check lighting, communication, and the ability to act on feedback.

V1 · 22:00

Flat fee + CPM bonus

Weekly decisions

Audit work consistently

Keep strong performers, coach fixable gaps, and stop repeating work that consistently misses the brief. Avoid basing the entire decision on one viral hit.

Once the message and format are repeatable, delegate management so output can grow while standards remain clear.

04 / The growth loop

Post. Listen. Make the next bet.

Publishing is the start of the experiment. The comments, product questions, and downstream actions help you decide what to make next.

The growth loop

Publish → listen → learn → repeat

Start the conversation

Reply where intent appears

Answer real product questions, explain the use case, and help people find the app. Viral reach does not automatically become downloads.

05 / Your first 30 days

A month of making. A system for learning.

The second video sets a goal of 1,000 app downloads in 30 days. Treat that as a target to test, not a promised result. This plan combines the two breakdowns into a practical sequence.

Explore the branches

Your first 30 days

A learning plan, not a growth promise

01

Days 1–3

Find the angle

Pick one audience and problem. Explore the niche’s feed and comments; collect phrases and questions.

Output: three hooks

Choose three pain points and turn them into specific openings for founder-led content.

02

Days 4–14

Make and listen

Try all three content formats. Test a sustainable posting cadence, answer questions, and record repeated objections.

Output: evidence of interest

Identify which problems lead people to ask about the app, not just which clips get views.

03

Days 15–21

Repeat the promising parts

Inspect videos that beat your baseline. Test new hooks around the same useful payoff and review available install data.

Output: a repeatable angle

If the message is clear, write a creator brief and consider a week-long paid trial. Hiring is optional at this stage.

04

Days 22–30

Build the next loop

Review the work, document the strongest formats, and decide what to repeat, revise, or stop. Delegate only when you can explain what works.

Output: next month’s experiment

Name the audience + problem + format that created meaningful interest, and what happened after people discovered the app.
